# Cinema environments

The 360° images the visionOS cinema wraps around the viewer. Each is rendered in Blender from a script here, so it can be rebuilt and changed, and so we know where every part of it came from.

Run `build.sh` to render them at full size and copy them into `Packages/CinemaContent` as HEIC. Run `build.sh --preview` for a quick 2048-wide render in `out/`. It needs Blender 5.2 or later in /Applications and [uv](https://docs.astral.sh/uv/), which fetches Pillow, numpy and resvg-py for `make_textures.py`.

## How the scenes are built

- `room_base.py` is what every environment shares, whatever the room: the seated camera at the origin, the sky and light from an HDRI, the render settings, `anchor()` and `screen_anchor()`, the anchors file, and small helpers for building the scene. `render_room(build, hdri)` starts an empty scene, calls `build()`, renders and writes the anchors. The build fails if `build()` places no screen or the HDRI isn't in `cache/`.
- `tent_base.py` builds the tent on top of that: a 5.6 × 5.6 m frame tent, with the viewer in the middle about 2.8 m from every wall, with canvas walls, clear vinyl windows, a groundsheet floor, the string lights, the Meshtastic branding, the dusk forest outside, and the "screen" anchor on the front wall where the app puts its screen (an anchor only; nothing is drawn there). Its `Layout` says where the windows, banner, poster and plaque go.
- An environment script, such as `sar_tent.py`, adds its own furniture, lights and boards in a `furnish(layout)` function and calls `tent_base.render(furnish)`.

Blender's +Y is the image's center: where the screen goes, on the tent's plain front wall. +X is the viewer's right and Z is up, with the viewer at the origin at seated eye height.

### Adding an environment

See [Cinema Environments](../../docs/developer/cinema-environments.md) for what makes a place work in the cinema and the rules for its assets.

1. For a tent, copy `sar_tent.py` to `<name>.py` and change `furnish()`. Keep the front wall clear. To move the windows or the branding away from your furniture, pass your own `tent_base.Layout(...)` to `render()`; set an item to None to leave it out. `furnish()` gets the same layout.
   For any other room, write `<name>.py` with a `build()` that makes the room and calls `screen_anchor(center, rotation)` on a plain surface the viewer faces, and end it with `room_base.render_room(build, "<hdri file>")`. Like `sar_tent.py`, it adds its own folder to `sys.path` before importing from `room_base`; the guide has a starting script.
2. For each board the app should draw on, make a plane and call `anchor("<id>", plane, width, height)`. The plane's +X is the board's right and its +Z faces the viewer.
3. For a new HDRI, add `hdri <polyhaven-name> <resolution>` to `build.sh`. It downloads to `cache/<name>_<resolution>.hdr`.
4. Add `environment <name>` at the end of `build.sh` and run it. It writes `<name>.heic` and `<name>_anchors.json` into `Packages/CinemaContent`.
5. Add a case to `CinemaEnvironment` with `imageName` set to `<name>`.

Since the camera, eye height and axes come from `room_base.py`, the anchors an environment writes line up with its image the same way the tent's do.

## Search and rescue tent

`sar_tent.py` furnishes the tent as a search and rescue command post. The comms table is on the left, with the "Comms equipment provided by Meshtastic Solutions" plaque on the wall above it. The door is on the right, with a SenseCAP Solar Node on a tripod just past it (the app's own model of it, `Meshtastic/Resources/DeviceModels/seeed_solar.usdz`) and a blank print in a thin dark frame on the wall beside it, hung from the eave. The map table is on the back wall to the right, with a lit work lamp hanging over it. The Meshtastic banner hangs on the back wall, behind the viewer. Chirpy's poster is pinned to the front wall, to the left of the screen. There is a clear vinyl window high on each side wall, with its cover rolled up above it; the vinyl catches soft reflections of the string lights. The floor is a ripstop groundsheet with seams, fold creases and a darker band at the walls, and nothing on it but the tables, the tripod and the two whiteboards' stands. The whiteboard behind the viewer's left shoulder is blank: the app draws the live team roster over it. The print is blank too: the app draws the latest trace route's share card on it. A second, portrait whiteboard stands at the front wall to the right of the screen: the app draws the nodes' status messages over it. A hidden light on each makes the painted board as bright as the panel the app draws, so the frame around the panel matches it.

The render also writes `sar_tent_anchors.json`, which `build.sh` copies next to the image. It says where each board the app draws on sits, relative to the camera, in RealityKit's axes. The app places its panels from it, so moving a board and re-rendering moves the panel with it. The app pushes each panel out along the lines from the camera until it sits just inside the backdrop sphere, and grows it to match, so it covers the same part of the view and has the same stereo depth as the painted tent around it.

## Ham shack

`ham_shack.py` builds an amateur radio shack on `room_base.py`, with no tent: a 5.4 × 5.4 m room, every wall 2.7 m from the viewer, with knotty pine paneling, a dark green carpet and a flush ceiling light. The front wall is plain plaster for the screen, with Chirpy's poster to its left and a portrait whiteboard, net control's check-in board, to its right: the app draws the nodes' status messages on it.

- **Left wall:** the operating desk runs along it. On the desk:
  - an HF radio with its amber display on the 20 meter FT8 frequency
  - a power supply and an SWR meter
  - a desk mic and a straight key
  - a computer running FT8
  - the open log and an arm lamp
  - three Meshtastic radios, from the app's own models at real size: a T-Deck, a T1000-E and a WisMesh Pocket

  Coax runs up the wall to a feedthrough panel. Above the back of the desk is a shelf with two stacks of radios, each on a different band, with headphones hung under it and round UTC and local clocks above it. The whiteboard the app draws the team roster on is above the front of the desk, and a band plan chart hangs past the far end of the desk.
- **Right wall:** a paneled door, then a double-hung window looking out on the dusk forest and a lattice tower with a beam and a vertical. The print the app draws the latest trace route on hangs between the door and the front corner.
- **Back wall:** the Meshtastic banner and a corkboard of QSL cards over a bookcase of made-up ham books (*SWR: It's Fine*, *The Joy of Coax*, *Morse Code in 30 Years* and more, by authors like Anne Tenna and Watt Ever), with four stood face out on top. The titles and authors are in `ham_books.py`.

The QSL cards, the clock dials, the radio displays, the meter faces, the FT8 screen, the band plan and the book spines and covers are drawn by `make_textures.py`. The QSL cards and the FT8 screen show no call signs, so none belongs to a real station.

## Fire lookout

`fire_lookout.py` builds a fire lookout on `room_base.py`, with no tent: a 4.6 × 4.6 m glass-walled cab on a 34 m tower on a summit, with a low wall all round, windows above it on every side, a wide sill shelf under them, a half-glass door in the back corner (the low wall and the sill stop at it, so it shows from the floor up, with Chirpy's poster on its lower panel, and the sills either side of it taper off on a diagonal so they don't stick out across the doorway), and a catwalk with a railing outside, low enough that the sill hides it from the seat. The storm shutters are all propped open as awnings, tipped up more steeply than the line of sight through the top of the glass from the seat, so they stay out of the view; the eaves are short so the shutters clear them. The screen hangs in the two middle bays in front, their outer posts set in so two thirds of each is behind the screen's bezel and the bays either side show more of the view, the whiteboards the app draws the team roster and the nodes' status messages on in two bays on the left, and the print it draws the latest trace route on in one on the right, all with the view round them. Inside are the fire finder, a cot with a sleeping bag and pillow, a box stove on a hearth pad with split firewood stacked beside it, a stool, a lit kerosene lantern, and on the sill binoculars, a radio and a T-Echo. The left sill and the catwalk outside are kept clear.

The image is a cutout (`render_room(..., cutout=True)`): the sunset HDRI lights the cab but isn't painted, so the image is transparent through the glass, reflections and all. The app draws a sky sphere behind it: `fire_lookout_sky.heic`, the view from the tower that `forest_view.py` renders (forested slopes falling away to valleys and blue ridges, under the clear sunset sky below); or, when it draws real terrain from the elevation tiles downloaded for the spatial map, `fire_lookout_puresky.heic`, a clear sunset sky turned so its sun is in the same place (each sun was found by rendering the sky alone and looking for its brightest pixel). The app shades that terrain to the same sun (`CinemaEnvironment.sun`), and takes the cab's front to face 156° true, so the sunset is in the west-northwest.

## Castle tower

`castle_tower.py` builds the open roof of a medieval square keep on `room_base.py`: a 6 m square of flagstones inside a low crenellated parapet of dressed stone, its merlons topping out about 6° below level from the seat, like the fire lookout's sill, so the horizon and the valleys show over them. Each corner is open, on a round bartizan corbelled out below it, and there is no roof. The screen stands in front on an oak frame with iron straps. The app's boards are scrolls: the team roster and the nodes' status messages hang from one oak gallows frame on the left, the latest trace route from one on the right. Only the frames, hooks and cords are painted; the app draws each scroll, so a scroll rolled up shows the view behind it. Behind the seat are an iron-strapped trapdoor down into the tower, a lit brazier, an oak bench, and a pole on the back-left bartizan flying the Meshtastic banner (`make_textures.py` draws it from the app's mark and wordmark).

`--stone gray` or `--stone pink` picks the stone; `build.sh` renders both, as `castle_tower_gray.heic` and `castle_tower_pink.heic`, with one `castle_tower_anchors.json`. Like the lookout, the image is a cutout lit by Kiara 8 at the same turn, so the app shows the lookout's skies and real terrain behind it.

## Lighthouse

`lighthouse.py` builds a lighthouse's lantern room on `room_base.py`, with no tent: a twelve-sided room glazed all round, its glass 2.4 m from the seat across the middle of each pane, a corner straight ahead so the two front panes meet behind the screen. The iron parapet under the glass is low (0.62 m), with a raised panel in each bay and a dark plinth along the floor, so the sea below shows over the wide sill down to about 14° below level. The gallery railing outside is kept under that line, so it never crosses the water in front of the nodes the app marks out there. Over the glass is a cream cornice ring and a shallow dome on dark ribs rising to the ventilator; outside, the dome's red cornice hangs a deep lip over the glass, which shows as a red band along the top of every pane. The floor is cast iron plates in rings and spokes, painted red oxide and worn.

- **Behind the seat:** the third-order Fresnel lens on its fluted cast iron pedestal: a belt of refracting prisms round the middle and rings of reflecting prisms narrowing above and below, in a brass frame, with the lamp lit inside. To its left the open hatch down to the watch room, its cover thrown back on the floor and the ladder's rails coming up through it; to its right a stool and a brass oil can.
- **Front:** the screen across the two front panes.
- **Left:** the whiteboards the app draws the team roster and the nodes' status messages on, in the panes 45° and 75° round, in thin dark frames.
- **Right:** the print the app draws the latest trace route on, 75° round, binoculars on the sill near the screen, and further round the VHF radio, a T-Echo and the logbook. The door out to the gallery is in the back right pane, half-glazed with a taller solid panel than the parapet and Chirpy's poster on it.

The image is a cutout like the fire lookout's. It's lit by the Kiara 8 sunset turned 129° (the lookout's turned 126° more), which puts the sun low over the sea on the viewer's left, a little behind, lighting the room through the left panes and keeping its glare away from the screen. Behind the glass the app draws `lighthouse_sky.heic`, the view from the lantern that `sea_view.py` renders: open sea on every side but behind, where the headland the tower stands on, 30 m above the water, reaches back to a coast of low hills, under the clear sunset sky turned to match. The sea is glossy deep water with swell and chop that calm with distance, rougher the farther it is, so the sun lays a path across it. Or, behind real terrain, `lighthouse_puresky.heic`, that clear sky alone. The lighthouse stands on a headland the app finds on the downloaded terrain's coast (`LookoutCoast`), its floor 24 m up (the lookout's is 34 m), turned to face the most open water; `CinemaEnvironment.sun` puts the terrain's sun at 260° from the front.
## City rooftop

`city_rooftop.py` builds the roof deck of a tall downtown building on `room_base.py`, with no tent: a 7.2 m square of wood decking tiles, its edges 3.6 m from the seat on the sides and behind, on a low curb with a glass railing round them. From the seat the deck hides the city below about 17° under level, and the glass hides nothing, so the city shows past the railing down to the streets a few blocks out. In front is the stair bulkhead, a stucco box 5.2 m wide and 2.95 m tall whose front wall, 2.7 m out, carries the screen, with the whiteboard the app draws the team roster on to its left and the print it draws the latest trace route on to its right, each lit by a gooseneck lamp. A steel door with a lit wall pack is on the bulkhead's left side. An A-frame sandwich board stands on the deck to the right, turned to the seat: its blank slate is the status board, standing portrait. Behind the seat are a wooden water tower on braced steel legs behind the left shoulder, a packaged HVAC unit behind the right, and between them a mast on a ballasted roof mount with a Yagi at the top, pointing out over the back railing, and the app's Solar Node (`seeed_solar.usdz`) on an arm halfway up, its panel turned to the seat. Festoon lights run from the bulkhead's top corners to poles at the back corners and across over the seat. A folding chair stands beside the seat on the left.

The image is a cutout, lit by the "Kloppenheim 06 (Pure Sky)" HDRI at the turn the lookout's skies use, so the sun is behind the viewer's right shoulder and the app's clear sky, `fire_lookout_puresky.heic`, and its terrain shading (`CinemaEnvironment.sun`) fit it unchanged. Behind real buildings the app shows that clear sky. With none, it shows `city_rooftop_sky.heic`, which `city_view.py` renders: a made-up downtown seen from 150 m up, blocks on a grid turned off the view, towers downtown and lower buildings outward, a window every 1.6 m on each 3.6 m floor with about one in five lit, and the street grid glowing amber out to a hazy horizon, under the same sky. No real skyline is copied.

## Space station

`space_cupola.py` builds a space station module on `room_base.py`, with no HDRI: the world is black and gives no light (`render_room(build, None, cutout=True)`), so the module is lit only by its own light strips and by the light through the cupola. The module is 4.6 m across and 4.9 m long, wider than a real node so its walls are at the distance the image reads well at, with its four long corners cut across at 45° for the utility runs and the light strips. Racks of off-white closeout panels line both sides, with fasteners, labels, drawer pulls, velcro, stowage bags, two experiment boxes with status lights, and blue handrails on standoffs. The deck has seat track and foot loops out by the racks, and nothing under the seat, since lines there bend into arcs across the view.

Overhead and a little ahead of the seat, the cupola opens out of the ceiling on a round throat: six trapezoid windows leaning in round a round window at the top, in deep frames, with handles under them. Its glass is clear, so the image is transparent through it. The screen hangs on the plain front bulkhead, under the ceiling. The whiteboards the app draws the team roster and the nodes' status messages on are strapped to the left racks and the print it draws the latest trace route on to the right ones, each with a hidden light that makes it as bright as the panel the app draws. Two laptops on swing arms by the front bulkhead show a crew schedule and the station's ground track. Behind the print on the right is the ham radio station: a placard with the station's frequencies, the radio with its display on the 145.800 MHz voice downlink, a hand mic on a coiled cord and a headset, with a T-Echo (the app's own model) strapped beside it. Behind the seat are the hatch to the next module, the Meshtastic mission patch over it, Chirpy's poster taped beside it, cargo bags and overhead stowage under a net, and at the front corners a fire extinguisher and a breathing mask's bag under red labels.

`space_earth.py` renders the view out of the windows, `space_earth.heic`: Earth from 420 km, centered up and behind the seat, so its edge crosses the cupola's front windows with black space below it. The ground is the Blue Marble, lit by a sun low on the left, with procedural clouds, a glow of air at the limb that turns red toward the night side, the Black Marble's city lights on the night side, and the sun's disk. `orbit.py` holds where Earth and the sun are, which both scripts use, so the light in the cupola comes from where the view puts Earth. The scene is in kilometers. Earth is overhead because the cupola is: the image is only transparent through the cupola's windows.

In the app, Earth behind the cupola is a real globe the app turns, and `space_earth.heic` is only shown if the globe's textures can't be loaded. `earth_textures.py` makes those textures, which `build.sh` writes as HEIC beside the painted view: `space_earth_ground.heic`, the Blue Marble at 4096 × 2048, lifted a little as `space_earth.py` lifts it; `space_earth_lights.heic`, the Black Marble at the same size, its countryside glow pushed down and tinted sodium orange; and `space_earth_clouds.heic`, NASA's Blue Marble cloud layer as gray, 2048 × 1024, the size NASA publishes it, which the app uses as the cloud shell's opacity. All three are equirectangular, longitude -180 at the left and north at the top, and together about 2.5 MB. `CinemaOrbit.swift` puts Earth and the sun where `orbit.py` does; the night side's sun is just past Earth's edge on the left, as seen from the seat, so the ground under the station is dark with a thin dawn along that edge.

## Sky deck

`sky_deck.py` builds a glass box cantilevered off the face of one of the tallest towers downtown on `room_base.py`: 6 m across and 5.4 m out from the tower's face, with the seat 2.2 m out. Its floor is glass from just behind the seat to the front wall, a pane in each bay of a grid of slim steel beams kept clear of the line straight down from the seat, with two deep girders under the side walls carrying the box out from the tower; behind the glass the floor is solid back to the core. The front and side walls are glass from floor to ceiling between slim mullions, with a low steel handrail 15 cm inside them. The ceiling is solid with recessed downlights, then a glass strip over the front, through which a mast on the tower's roof shows with the app's Solar Node (`seeed_solar.usdz`) on an arm near its top. Behind the seat is the tower's core, a solid wall with the lift doors, the Meshtastic banner and Chirpy's poster, and the tower's face, dark glass in pale mullions, falls away below it to the street and runs out past the box either side. The screen hangs in front on a plain panel on two rods from the ceiling, 2.65 m out, with the whiteboard the app draws the team roster on to its left, the slate the status board is drawn on to its right, and the print for the latest trace route on the left wall, each hung on two rods and turned to the seat.

The image is a cutout, lit as the city rooftop is, so it shares the rooftop's skies and terrain shading: past the glass, the floor's included, the app puts its clear sky and the real city, or `city_rooftop_sky.heic` with none, whose streets show under the floor.

## HEIC

`build.sh` converts the rendered PNG with `sips` at HEIC quality 88. That quality was picked by decoding the HEIC and comparing it with the PNG (PSNR and SSIM, over the whole image and over crops of the canvas, the banner, the plaque, the forest through the door and a window), and by looking at the same crops side by side. Quality 88 matched or beat the old JPEG at quality 85 in every crop, and no difference from the PNG could be seen in the crops at 3× zoom. For the tent at 4096 × 2048:

| | Size | PSNR (whole / forest through the door) | SSIM (whole / forest) |
|---|---|---|---|
| PNG | 8.5 MB | | |
| JPEG 85 (before) | 653 KB | 47.9 / 43.5 dB | 0.9922 / 0.976 |
| HEIC 80 | 235 KB | 47.6 / 42.3 dB | 0.9920 / 0.971 |
| HEIC 88 | 352 KB | 48.2 / 43.6 dB | 0.9926 / 0.978 |

`sips` gives the same file for every quality from 80 to 85.

- The topo map, the whiteboard and the ham shack's cards, clocks, displays, meters and chart are drawn by `make_textures.py`.
- The banner, poster and plaque use the app's own artwork, rasterized from its SVGs by `make_textures.py` with resvg (the `resvg-py` package; chosen because it needs no system libraries, unlike cairosvg, and keeps transparency, unlike `qlmanage`): the Meshtastic mark (`Meshtastic/Resources/AppIcon.icon/Assets/Meshtastic Logo.svg`), the wordmark (`meshtastic-wordmark-white.imageset`), Chirpy (`Chirpy.imageset`) and the Meshtastic Solutions mark (`MeshtasticSolutionsMark.imageset`). The plaque follows the layout of the one in the visionOS Hardware Showcase.
- The forest seen through the door and the windows is the "Sunset Forest" HDRI from Poly Haven (https://polyhaven.com/a/sunset_forest), released under CC0. `build.sh` downloads it into `cache/`.
- The lookout's cab is lit by the "Kiara 8 Sunset" HDRI from Poly Haven (https://polyhaven.com/a/kiara_8_sunset), CC0; the sky over the forest view and behind real terrain is "Kloppenheim 06 (Pure Sky)" from Poly Haven (https://polyhaven.com/a/kloppenheim_06_puresky), CC0.
- The city rooftop's deck, bulkhead, water tower, HVAC unit, mast and furniture are modeled in `city_rooftop.py`, and its painted city in `city_view.py`. It is lit by "Kloppenheim 06 (Pure Sky)", as above.

- The display of the lookout's radio on its sill is drawn by `make_textures.py`. The T-Echo and the Solar Node are the app's own models.
- The lighthouse, its lens and its furniture are modeled in `lighthouse.py`, and the sea and the headland in `sea_view.py`. It reuses the lookout's radio display, Chirpy's poster and the whiteboard texture, and is lit and skied by the same two Poly Haven HDRIs as the lookout.
- The space station's mission patch (with the Meshtastic mark), ham radio placard and display, and laptop screens are drawn by `make_textures.py`. The ground track's map is made from the Blue Marble.
- Earth in the view out of the cupola is NASA's Blue Marble Next Generation, July 2004, with topography and bathymetry (NASA Earth Observatory, Reto Stöckli; https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/), and its city lights are NASA's Black Marble 2016 (NASA Earth Observatory, Joshua Stevens and Miguel Román). Both are public domain. `build.sh` downloads them into `cache/` from assets.science.nasa.gov and cuts the Blue Marble down to 16384 wide, the largest texture Blender's GPU render takes. The clouds in the painted view are procedural; the app's globe uses the Blue Marble 2002 cloud layer (`cloud_combined_2048.jpg`, NASA Earth Observatory, Reto Stöckli; public domain), which `build.sh` downloads from the Blue Marble feature's images on assets.science.nasa.gov.

## Banana for scale

`banana_for_scale.py` builds the banana that stands beside a model on the stand, and beside it in the iPhone and iPad AR view, at real size, from `drawings/banana_icon.svg` ("Banana icon" by Keistutis on [Openclipart](https://openclipart.org/detail/189457/banana), CC0). It renders the drawing without its shadow, stands it on end with the stem up, inflates the outline into a round banana 18.5 cm tall, and lays the drawing on its front and back. The front gets an oval white label with the Meshtastic logo (`Meshtastic/Assets.xcassets/logo-black.imageset`), halfway up and turned to run along the banana, with the logo upright; the back has its own copy of the drawing without it. Run `uv run scripts/cinema/banana_for_scale.py`; it writes `Meshtastic/Resources/DeviceModels/banana_for_scale.usdz`, in the app rather than the cinema's package so iOS ships it too.
