Cinema (Apple Vision Pro)

On Apple Vision Pro, the Cinema puts the app on a big screen inside a search and rescue comms tent, a ham shack, a fire lookout, on top of a castle tower, in a lighthouse, on a city rooftop, in a space station or in a sky deck. Open it with the tent button in the round glass menu under the app's tab bar. The app's window closes while you're in the tent and comes back when you leave.

The screen

The screen on the tent's front wall shows the app. The tabs above it are the app's own: Messages, Nodes, Map, Settings and Connect. It opens on Connect. Sheets, menus and dialogs that would pop up over the app open as pages beside the screen instead. Things that only work in the app's window, like sharing or saving a file, show an Open in App Window button in their place. While the radio is locked down or needs a firmware update, the screen says to continue in the app window.

The 3D map

The 3D spatial map opens in front of you when you enter, at its largest size. If you close it, Open 3D Map on the control panel brings it back.

Radio models

On a node's details, the AR button on the radio's 3D model stands the model on a clear stand in front of you, at real size. Pinch it to pick it up and turn it over; let go and it goes back on the stand. The card beside it shows the node's name, when it was last heard, its battery, signal and hops. Pinch the bar under the stand to move it; the stand, the model and the card turn to face you wherever it goes, like the card on the 3D map. Banana for Scale on the card stands a real-size banana, about 18.5 cm tall with its stem up and a Meshtastic sticker on its front, on its own plate beside the model, and grows with the model's size slider so the comparison stays true. The X on the card puts the model away. Outside the tent, the AR button places the model in the room around you instead.

For the radio you're connected to, the model shows its screen live, the same picture as the Device Screen view. The card then has a size slider, from real size up to five times it so the screen is easy to read from the seat, and the radio's buttons: four directions around OK, with Back underneath.

Boards

  • Team Favorites, on the whiteboard: your favorite nodes and the connected radio, with when each was last heard, its battery, signal, hops and distance from the radio. Tap a node to center the 3D map on it and open its details on the screen. With more favorites than fit, pinch and drag the list to scroll it.
  • The latest trace route, on the framed print (by the door in the tent, beside the window in the ham shack): the same card the trace route widget shows, for the last trace route you sent or heard. Tap it to show the route on the screen's map and fly it on the 3D map.
  • Status Board, on the portrait whiteboard beside the screen: your radio's own status message, then the nodes heard with one, latest first, as many as fit, with a count of the rest. Tap a node to center the 3D map on it and open its details on the screen. Set your radio's status from its node details.

Fire lookout

The third place is a fire lookout: a glass cab on a tall tower on a summit at sunset, looking out over forested ridges, with its storm shutters propped open as awnings all round, the screen hanging in the front windows, the team whiteboard in a bay on the left and the trace route print in one on the right, with the view all round them.

If you've downloaded terrain for the 3D map, the view out the windows can be the real thing. The control panel's Summit picker lists up to 20 of the highest mountains around the mesh, highest first, with how far each is from your radio and which way. Pick one and the cab stands on it, with the terrain around it out every window, lit by the same sunset. Where the ground is forest, conifers cover it like the painted view's forest: single trees on the slopes close to the cab, big trees in front where the first forest shows from a high summit, then rows of trees out to about 15 km, hazing toward the gray of the far ridges. Far trees are drawn larger than life, so they stay about as big in view as the painting's and still read as trees from a high, steep summit. Only ground that shows over the sill and isn't behind a nearer ridge gets trees. Every node with a position is marked out the windows where it really is from there, by bearing and distance: its round chip on a stem over the spot. Where markers would cover each other, some stand on taller stems so each node keeps its own; only where there's no room left do nodes share one marker, with how many there are on it. A node behind the screen or a board is out of sight.

Tap a marker to open its card beside it, so you don't have to turn to the screen: the node's name, how far away it is and its bearing, its altitude, when it was last heard, its battery, signal and hops. Show on Map centers the 3D map on the node, and Show on Screen opens its details on the screen. A marker that stands for several nodes lists them first; pick one to see its card. The card opens beside the marker as you see it, at reading distance; pinch the bar under it to move it. Tap the marker again, or the card's close button, to close it. Forest sunset puts the forest view back.

While the cab stands on a summit, the 3D map shows the lookout there too: the cab on its braced timber tower, facing the way the screen does. If a node stands on the same summit, the lookout stands just beside it.

Castle tower

The fourth place is the open top of a medieval castle tower on a summit at sunset: a stone roof with low battlements all round, so the horizon and the valleys below show over them, open corners on round turrets, and the Meshtastic banner flying behind you. Pick Gray or Pink stone on the control panel.

Everything the fire lookout does, the castle does too: the same Summit picker and Turn Lookout dial, the real terrain with its forest, and a marker for every node, with its card. With no roof and no windows, the view is open all round.

The boards are parchment scrolls hanging from oak frames: Team Favorites and the Status Board on the frame to your left, the latest trace route on the one to your right. Tap a scroll's top or bottom roller to roll it up out of the way, and see the view behind it; tap a roller again to let it down. Each scroll stays as you left it.

While the castle stands on a summit, the 3D map shows a castle tower on the summit in the stone you picked, in place of the lookout.

Lighthouse

The fifth place is the lantern room at the top of a lighthouse on a headland at sunset: glass all round above a low iron parapet, so the sea shows below you on every side, with the sun going down over the water on your left. The Fresnel lens stands behind you on its iron pedestal, lit, beside the hatch down to the watch room and the door out to the gallery. The screen hangs in the two front panes, the two whiteboards in panes on the left and the trace route print in one on the right.

If you've downloaded terrain for the 3D map along a coast, the view can be the real thing. The control panel's Headland picker lists up to 20 places on the coast around the mesh that look out over the most open sea, each with which way it faces, its height, and how far it is from your radio and which way. Pick one and the lighthouse stands on it, turned so the screen faces the open water, with the real coast and sea out every pane and a marker for every node, with its card, as in the fire lookout. Turn it with the Turn Lighthouse dial, which works like the lookout's. Sea view puts the painted sea back. Without terrain along a coast, the panel says so and the painted sea stays.

While the lighthouse stands on a headland, the 3D map shows a lighthouse there: a white tower with a railed gallery and a lit lantern under a red dome, facing the way the screen does.

City rooftop

The sixth place is the roof deck of a tall downtown building at sunset: wood decking with a glass railing round its edges, so the city shows past it on three sides, a wooden water tower and an air conditioning unit behind you, string lights overhead, and an antenna mast with a Yagi and a Solar Node. The screen is on the front wall of the stairwell, with Team Favorites to its left and the latest trace route to its right. The Status Board is the sandwich board standing on the deck to your right.

If you've downloaded a map with terrain for the 3D map, the city past the railing can be the real one. The control panel's Rooftop picker lists up to 12 of the tallest buildings around the mesh whose heights are in the map, tallest first, with how far each is from your radio and which way. Pick one and the deck stands on its roof, with the real buildings out to about 3.5 km round it, standing on the real terrain, and the terrain beyond, lit by the same sunset. Every node with a position gets a marker and a card, as from the fire lookout, and the Turn Rooftop dial turns the view. City skyline puts the painted city back, which is also what shows with no map downloaded.

While the deck stands on a roof, the 3D map shows a small water tower and the mast with its Yagi on that roof, on top of the building when Buildings is on. The panel's Show Rooftop button opens the 3D map zoomed all the way in on that roof, with Buildings turned on, so you can find it among the towers.

Space station

The sixth place is a module of a space station in orbit, 420 km up. The module is lined with racks of off-white panels, with handrails, stowed bags and laptops on swing arms. Above you and a little ahead, the cupola opens out of the ceiling: six windows round a round one at the top, with Earth through them and the sunlit ground under the station. Over the front windows you can see Earth's edge, with its thin blue line of air and black space beyond. The screen is on the plain front bulkhead. Team Favorites and the Status Board are strapped to the racks on your left, and the latest trace route to the racks on your right. Behind the print are the station's ham radio, tuned to its voice downlink, and a Meshtastic radio strapped beside it. Behind you are the hatch to the next module, the mission patch and Chirpy's poster.

Earth through the cupola is a real globe, not a painting: the ground drifts by under the station as it flies, at the speed the real station goes round Earth (once every 92 minutes), with the clouds drifting over it a little faster. The control panel sets which side of Earth is under you and how fast it goes by. By day the ground under the station is in sunlight; by night it's the dark side, with the lights of towns and cities and a thin dawn along one edge of Earth. With Reduce Motion on, Earth holds still.

The station has no summit and no real terrain, so the Summit picker and the Turn Lookout dial don't show there.

While the cinema is in the space station, the 3D map shows the station floating high over the middle of the mesh, above the highest ground and buildings, slowly circling it. It isn't where the real station is. With Reduce Motion on, it holds still.

Sky deck

The seventh place is a glass box hung off the face of one of the tallest towers downtown, at the top. Its floor is glass from just behind your seat to the front wall, on a grid of slim steel beams, so you can look straight down past your feet to the streets far below. Its front and side walls are glass from floor to ceiling, with a low handrail inside them. Behind you is the tower's core, with the lift doors, the Meshtastic banner and Chirpy's poster. The screen hangs from the ceiling in front of you, with Team Favorites to its left, the Status Board to its right and the latest trace route on the left wall, all hung clear of the glass. Over the front of the box the ceiling is glass, and through it you can see a mast on the tower's roof with a Solar Node on it.

If you've downloaded a map with terrain for the 3D map, the city below and around can be the real one. The control panel's Tower picker lists the tallest buildings around the mesh, as the rooftop's does, each with which way its deck looks out and how tall it is. The deck hangs off the face of the tower whose view the other tall towers block least, turned so the screen looks out from it, with the real buildings round it standing on the real terrain, right down to the street under the floor. Turn the view with the Turn Sky Deck dial. City skyline puts the painted city back, which is also what shows with no map downloaded.

While the deck hangs off a tower, the 3D map shows a small glass box on that tower's face, just under its roof, looking out the way the deck does. Show Tower opens the 3D map zoomed all the way in on it, with Buildings turned on.

Control panel

  • Environment: the place the screen is set in: the search and rescue tent, or a ham shack, a wood-paneled radio room with an operating desk, a window out to the antenna tower, and the same boards; its status board is net control's check-in board, standing portrait beside the screen; or a fire lookout, a glass cab on a summit at sunset; or a castle tower, an open stone roof on a summit at sunset; or a lighthouse, a glass lantern room over the sea at sunset; or a city rooftop, a roof deck over downtown at sunset; or a space station, a module under its cupola with Earth through the windows; or a sky deck, a glass-floored box hung off a tower, looking down at the city.
  • Stone: on the castle tower, gray or pink.
  • Summit: in the fire lookout or on the castle tower, with terrain downloaded, which summit it stands on, or the forest sunset.
  • Headland: in the lighthouse, with terrain along a coast downloaded, which headland it stands on, or the sea view.
  • Rooftop: on the city rooftop, with terrain downloaded, which building it stands on, or the painted city skyline.
  • Tower: in the sky deck, with terrain downloaded, which tower it hangs off, or the painted city skyline.
  • Show Rooftop (Show Tower in the sky deck): with a building picked, opens the 3D map zoomed in on its roof with Buildings on.
  • Turn Lookout, Turn Lighthouse, Turn Rooftop or Turn Sky Deck: with a summit, headland, rooftop or tower picked, a compass dial for which way the room faces, so the screen looks out over another part of the view. Tap one of its sixteen marks, a sixteenth of a turn (22.5°) apart, or drag round it and let go. The eighths are drawn larger. The caption under it says which way the room faces. The model on the 3D map turns with it.
  • Side of Earth: in the space station, Day for the sunlit side under the cupola, or Night for the dark side with its city lights.
  • Orbit Speed: in the space station, how fast Earth goes by, from real time (1×) to ten times faster (10×).
  • Open 3D Map: shown while the 3D map is closed.
  • Move it: pinch the bar under the panel and drag it wherever you want it.
  • Leave: closes the tent and brings back the app's window.

The tent also closes when you leave the app.
